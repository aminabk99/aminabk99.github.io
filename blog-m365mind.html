Building M365Mind: ask your Microsoft 365 governance a question and get the real policy back

June 2026 · GitHub

Python · FastAPI · ChromaDB · BM25 · Cross-encoder reranking · Ollama

This project started as DocMind, a local RAG app for asking questions about PDFs. I kept working on it and it turned into something more specific: M365Mind, a tool that answers questions about Microsoft 365 governance policies, things like Conditional Access rules, Sensitivity Labels, and Named Locations, and gives you the exact policy back with a citation, not a paraphrase.

Most "chat with your docs" tools embed a question, grab the nearest vectors, and hand them to an LLM. I treated retrieval as the actual product instead of plumbing, because on a governance question the right policy matters more than fluent prose. Policy language is templated and keyword-heavy, words like "require," "block," "grant," exact label names, which pure vector search can wash out, while pure keyword search misses paraphrases. So I run both at once: dense vectors over ChromaDB and BM25 keyword search, each returning their top 20, then merge the two rankings with Reciprocal Rank Fusion. That gives me a strong shortlist without needing either score calibrated against the other.

The shortlist then goes through a cross-encoder that reads each question and policy pair together and scores real relevance, which a retriever working in isolation can't do. The reranked top results become the answer's sources. By default the answer is extractive, built straight from the top-ranked policy text with no model generation, so it comes back in well under a second. There's an optional "Explain with AI" mode that sends the same sources to a local Ollama model for a natural-language summary, and every citation the model produces gets checked against the actual retrieved sources before it's shown. The model isn't allowed to invent a source.

Every answer also carries a confidence score, and getting that honest took a real fix. The cross-encoder outputs an unbounded relevance score per policy, and my first version squashed it with an arbitrary linear formula that pinned almost every genuine match near 0.5, so even a perfect answer looked uncertain. I replaced that with a sigmoid, the actual calibrated inverse of a logit, applied to the average of the top two reranked scores. Strong matches now read as strong instead of hovering at a coin flip.

Hardest part: the confidence calibration bug was the kind of thing that doesn't throw an error, it just quietly makes every answer look less trustworthy than it is. I only caught it by actually reading the numbers next to answers I knew were correct and noticing they didn't match my own judgment of how good the retrieval was.

Most interesting part: building an eval set instead of trusting my own read of the outputs. I wrote governance test cases grounded in the demo policies, including a deliberately out-of-scope question that the system should refuse rather than answer, and score every run on faithfulness and relevance so quality is tracked over time instead of asserted once. Everything runs locally, embeddings, reranking, and generation, so no policy content leaves the machine.
