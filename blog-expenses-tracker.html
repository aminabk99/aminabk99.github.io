Building an enterprise expense tracker on Power Apps, then adding a real approval workflow

July 2026 · GitHub

Power Apps · Dataverse · Power Automate · PowerFx · Office 365

This is a business expense tracking app built on Microsoft Power Apps and Dataverse, where employees submit, review, and manage expenses from a single mobile-first interface. It has live spending dashboards, a monthly trend chart, quick-fill templates for common expense types, and three themes (Default, Dark, High Contrast) that switch with a single URL parameter instead of touching individual controls.

The theming works through two global variables set on app load: one full color palette with three variants per color, and a resolver that picks the right variant based on the active theme. Every control references the resolver instead of a hardcoded value, so changing the whole app's appearance is one parameter, not a pass through every screen.

Once the core tracker worked, I layered a self-service access request workflow on top of it. An employee requests Teams or SharePoint access from inside the app, their manager approves or rejects it through Power Automate's Approvals connector, and the system updates the request status automatically. There's no live Microsoft 365 tenant behind this build, so the actual grant is simulated by updating a Dataverse field, but the approval and branching logic runs exactly as it would against a real tenant. I tested both branches with two real requests, one approved and one rejected, and confirmed the flow updates the right status both ways, not just on the happy path.

Hardest part: Power Automate only gives you a single unified trigger for row changes, added, modified, or deleted, so I had to explicitly filter it down to "Added" to get the behavior I wanted. I lost progress on the flow once and had to rebuild it from scratch. The Update a Row action also exposed close to a dozen fields at once, including a confusing pair where my own custom Status field sat right next to Dataverse's built-in Status fields, so I had to carefully remove every field except mine on both branches to avoid writing to the wrong place.

Most interesting part: the quick-create templates on the home screen. Instead of making people navigate to the form and manually pick a category, the three most common expense types show up as one-tap shortcuts that pre-fill the category field. It's a small thing, but it's built entirely in PowerFx with no custom connectors, and it's the kind of friction reduction that real enterprise apps care about.
